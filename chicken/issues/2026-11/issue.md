# Chicken Monitor

**Issue 2: November 2026**
Field Assembly Market Publications · Information as of November 13, 2026 · Data run `2026-11-13T230637Z`

This is the second issue of the Chicken Monitor. It is written for a restaurant, caterer or food-service buyer facing a supplier's price claim on chicken. It reports market evidence. It does not estimate any supplier's cost and draws no conclusion about any contract.

## The read

Where this record can compare October with a year earlier, the producer and wholesale measures are higher than October 2025 and the one retail measure with a year-earlier value, an ERS composite, is lower. For five BLS consumer series, the two consumer price indexes and the three retail average prices, the comparison does not exist, because BLS published no October 2025 value for them.

The producer price index for slaughter chickens is 10.4% above October 2025, and the index for young chickens, whole and in parts, is 2.1% above. USDA's Economic Research Service puts its wholesale broiler composite at 92.1 cents a pound in October, 9.9% higher than a year earlier. All three are lower than two years earlier: against October 2024 the slaughter chicken index is 10.3% lower, the young chicken index 7.8% lower and the ERS wholesale composite 16.3% lower. The last issue reported all three below a year earlier for September; this issue's comparison is with a different month a year ago, and BLS has since revised the September slaughter chicken index upward. The uncertainty section says what that changes.

The monthly wholesale prices in USDA's National Chicken Report, which price individual cuts, still cannot be compared with a year earlier: this record holds no 2025 reading of that report. What it can show is the latest reporting month against the one before. In the block this record files as October, the four weeks from October 5 to October 30, boneless skinless breast averaged 115.49 cents a pound, against 121.18 in the September block, a fall of 4.7%. Three other rows were lower by less than 1%: whole wings by 0.7%, to 109.62 cents; tenderloins by 0.4%, to 112.65 cents; and bulk leg quarters by 0.4%, to 53.28 cents. Two were higher: drumsticks by 1.1%, to 56.11 cents, and the national composite whole bird by 0.4%, to 117.11 cents. These are wholesale prices for fresh domestic product, the parts priced FOB and the whole bird delivered. The explanation below gives what each row prices.

At retail, the ERS retail broiler composite, at 241.8 cents a pound, is 1.1% below October 2025. The consumer price index for chicken and the three retail average prices carry no year-earlier comparison this month: BLS marks October 2025 "Data unavailable due to the 2025 lapse in appropriations" for each of them. The average retail price of boneless chicken breast in October was $4.099 a pound, of a fresh whole chicken $2.026 and of bone-in chicken legs $1.747. Against October 2024, the comparison that does exist, the chicken consumer index is 1.0% higher, boneless breast 1.5% higher, whole chicken 2.0% higher and bone-in legs 4.5% lower. These are different measures from the wholesale prices and not one price read at different points in the chain, so the gap between the wholesale and retail moves is not a margin, and this record cannot say what it is made of.

Fewer birds were slaughtered than in the comparable week a year earlier. In the week ending November 7, 167,928 thousand young chickens were slaughtered under federal inspection, 3.9% fewer than in the comparable week the report gives for 2025, at an average live weight of 6.73 pounds against 6.65, 1.2% heavier. Year to date, head slaughtered is 1.4% higher. These are birds and live weight, not pounds of meat, and the record cannot say what they did to price.

What these figures establish is narrow. The two producer price indexes and the ERS wholesale composite are higher than in October 2025 and lower than in October 2024, so the direction of "the year" depends on which year is meant. The ERS retail composite is lower than a year ago. From the September to the October reporting month, the wholesale boneless skinless breast price fell 4.7%, three of the other five rows this issue carries fell by less than 1%, and drumsticks and the whole bird rose. These six are not the whole report: it prices other cuts, and they did not all move the same way; both thigh averages rose. The figures do not establish what happened to any particular supplier's costs.

## The explanation

Six kinds of measure appear above and they answer different questions. The report 3649 figures are weighted average wholesale prices for named cuts over a reporting month. The producer price indexes measure the selling prices received by domestic producers and exclude imports: one for chickens sold for slaughter, one for young chickens sold whole and in parts. The two consumer price indexes measure the change in prices urban consumers pay: one for chicken at retail, one for meals and snacks bought away from home, which is not a restaurant's cost. The three average prices are retail prices per pound for three items. The ERS figures are composites ERS computes, one wholesale and one retail, and the difference between them. The slaughter figures are counts of birds, not prices. None of them is a supplier's cost, and a movement in one does not fix a movement in another.

The report 3649 rows are narrower than the words "breast" or "wings" suggest. The report sets every row under a heading that gives the product group, the market, fresh or frozen, the word "Conventional", and the delivery basis, and each row here sits under "Domestic - Fresh - Conventional". For no cut does it state a grade, a pack, a trim or a size. It does not define "Conventional", and it does not spell out its abbreviations "B/S" and "WOG"; this issue reads "B/S" as boneless skinless, the usual trade meaning, but the report does not say so. Each row carries a price range, a weighted average and the pounds traded, and the report does not say what the average is weighted by or which sales it includes. The breast row is "Breast - B/S", not the line-run breast or the breast with ribs, which the report prices on rows of their own. The wing row is "Wings - Whole"; the report carries no row for wing portions, so this is not a price for cut wings. The drumstick row is not bone-in legs, which the report prices separately. The leg quarter row is "Leg quarters - Bulk", and the report does not say what the bulk pack is. None of these rows is frozen product.

The parts rows are priced FOB and the whole bird delivered, and the report names no shipping point for the parts rows and no destination for the national composite. So the whole bird and the parts are not priced on one delivery basis, and their levels should not be set against each other. The national composite whole bird is not a price for any one bird size, which the report gives on rows of its own. The report does not say what the composite combines; in the November 3 report, as in the one before it, its pounds equal those of the national composite WOG and whole-body rows added together, and its price is their pound-weighted average.

The report's month is a block of whole weeks, not a calendar month, and the report labels it only by its dates. The block this record files as October runs four weeks, from Monday, October 5 to Friday, October 30, and lies wholly inside calendar October. The one filed as September ran five weeks, from August 31 to October 2, so it took in the last day of August and the first two of October. This record files each block under the month holding most of its days. Because the two blocks differ in length, the pounds traded in them are not comparable, and this issue sets out only October's. The change column compares the two months' weighted averages and nothing else. The report for the October block is dated November 3.

The slaughter report counts young chickens slaughtered under federal inspection, in thousands of head, in four weight classes in pounds, with an average live weight for each class and for the total. In the week ending November 7 the four classes, lightest first, were 22,718, 45,204, 52,238 and 47,768 thousand head. The report says the lightest class is normally marketed bone-in into fast food and food service and may include Cornish hens, and the heaviest is normally marketed deboned or as roasters. A higher average live weight means more live weight for the same number of birds, but live weight is not ready-to-cook product, and the record holds no measure of meat produced.

The ERS composites are named in ERS's file as the wholesale broiler composite, the retail broiler composite and the wholesale-retail broiler spread, in cents per pound. The file does not say which cuts or weights make up either composite, or at what point the wholesale composite is priced. The wholesale composite is not the report 3649 national composite whole bird: the composite for calendar October was 92.1 cents a pound, and the whole bird in report 3649's October block 117.11. The spread is the retail composite less the wholesale one, 241.8 less 92.1, and it is 6.8% lower than a year earlier because the wholesale composite is higher than in October 2025 and the retail composite lower. Against October 2024 the same spread is 11.1% higher. That is arithmetic between two composites, not a finding about anyone's margin. ERS's file gives its retail composite an October 2025 value, 244.5 cents a pound, while marking the three BLS chicken retail prices for that month "NA". The file does not say how that composite was arrived at, so the 1.1% fall in the retail composite and the 6.8% fall in the spread rest on a year-earlier value this record cannot trace.

USDA's Food Price Outlook, in its update of October 23, 2026, puts poultry consumer prices 1.1% higher from September 2025 to September 2026, meats, poultry and fish 2.2% higher, food away from home 3.5% higher and all food 2.5% higher. Its forecast intervals for 2027 are −5.5% to 7.9% for poultry, −4.5% to 10.1% for meats, poultry and fish, 0.7% to 5.0% for food away from home, and −2.5% to 6.2% for all food. The poultry interval runs from a fall to a rise.

## The uncertainty

This is the second issue of this title. It compares the BLS, ERS and slaughter series with the same period a year earlier where the source holds one and, where the record holds it, two years earlier. It compares the report 3649 rows only with the reporting month before. Month-to-month movement in the BLS and ERS series is not in this issue's computed set, so nothing here measures their October against September, and this issue does not set its October figures for those series beside the September figures the last issue printed.

Eleven rows above have no year-earlier comparison, for two different reasons.

Five are BLS series: the consumer price indexes for chicken and for food away from home, and the retail average prices of boneless chicken breast, fresh whole chicken and bone-in chicken legs. BLS marks October 2025 "Data unavailable due to the 2025 lapse in appropriations" for all five, so the retained file holds no October 2025 value for them, and nothing in it says one will be published. Each has an October 2024 value, and the two-year changes above are computed from it. Both producer price indexes have an October 2025 value.

Six are the report 3649 rows: boneless skinless breast, tenderloins, whole wings, bulk leg quarters, drumsticks and the national composite whole bird. Each report prints its current month and, as "Previous Months Trading", the month before; the record reads each report's current month, and the earliest it reads is August 2026. A year-over-year figure for this series will first exist when the record holds the same reporting month a year apart: the August 2027 block, in the report due about the start of September 2027. Next year's block filed as August need not cover the same weeks, or the same number of weeks, as this year's. The comparison with the month before works now, and the table gives it.

The producer price comparison reads differently from the last issue's for reasons that are not all movement in October. This issue compares October with October 2025; the last compared September with September 2025, and both producer price indexes and the ERS wholesale composite stood lower in October 2025 than in September 2025. Separately, the September 2026 producer price indexes were preliminary when the last issue quoted them, and in this run's BLS file the September 2026 slaughter chicken index is higher than the value the last issue carried, by enough that the year-over-year fall the last issue reported for it is close to nil on the revised value. The September 2026 young chicken index changed only slightly. The revised September values are not in this issue's computed set, so it prints none of them.

The October producer price indexes are preliminary, and BLS may revise them for up to four months after first publication.

Each report 3649 average rests on the pounds traded behind it, and those differ widely. Of the six rows here, whole wings traded the fewest pounds in October, 1,805 thousand, and the national composite whole bird the most, 27,466 thousand. Each change is one month's average against another's and says nothing about how prices moved within either month.

This issue carries no thigh figure: neither thigh row is in its computed set. The report prices bone-in and boneless thighs on two rows. The bone-in thigh row again trades the fewest pounds of any row in the report, so its average rests on very little volume. The report of October 2 printed a low end of the boneless thigh price range that was most likely a misprint; the report of November 3 prints no range for any row this issue carries, or for either thigh row, that looks like one. This issue quotes no price range.

The slaughter week ending November 7 is marked preliminary, and the record holds the figure as first published. The report's year-ago figures are for a "comparable week" it does not date, and its year-to-date totals compare the two years over comparable periods as the report states them. This record holds nine weeks of the slaughter report, so the year-over-year figures above are the report's own, not this record's.

The three retail average prices are U.S. city averages for boneless chicken breast, fresh whole chicken and bone-in chicken legs. They are what shoppers paid at retail, not a foodservice price and not a price for a particular pack or brand.

The USDA Food Price Outlook figures are year-over-year from September 2025 to September 2026, not October to October, and its poultry category covers more than chicken. The forecast intervals are for 2027 and cover categories, not chicken.

The report 3649 prices are averages of reported trade, not an offer available to any particular buyer, and a restaurant's delivered cost includes processing, freight and terms this record does not see.

## The buyer's question

Ask the supplier three things before accepting a chicken increase.

Which product and which basis: the cut, whether it is fresh or frozen, how it is packed, and whether the price is FOB or delivered. A whole-wing price is not a price for cut wings, and a drumstick price is not a bone-in leg price. In the report 3649 rows above, the parts are priced FOB and the whole bird delivered, and none of them is the price of a case of any product.

Which dates: the month or week the original quote priced from and the one the adjustment references, and, for a claim about "the year", which month a year ago. Report 3649's months are blocks of whole weeks, and the block this record files as October ran from October 5 to October 30. Boneless skinless breast went from 121.18 to 115.49 cents a pound between the September and October months; a quote priced from one month can sit some way from the next. The producer price indexes are higher than October 2025 and lower than October 2024.

Which series: the published series or supplier invoice behind the stated increase. Every series above is free and public. Against a year earlier, both chicken producer price indexes and the ERS wholesale composite are higher, and the ERS retail composite is 1.1% lower. The chicken consumer price index and the three retail average prices have no October 2025 value, so a claim about their change on the year cannot be checked against them for this month; against October 2024 the retail average price of boneless breast is 1.5% higher and of bone-in legs 4.5% lower. This record holds no year-earlier figure for the report 3649 cut prices, so a claim that a wholesale cut price rose on the year cannot be checked against it here. A claim about the latest month can be: of the six rows above, four were lower in the October block than in the September one and two were higher.
