# Lobster Monitor

**Issue 2: November 2026**
Field Assembly Market Publications · Information as of November 16, 2026 · Data run `2026-11-17T015818Z`

This issue is written for anyone facing a supplier's lobster price claim: an event client with an escalation clause, a planner comparing quotes, a small buyer asked to accept an increase. It reports market evidence. It does not estimate any supplier's cost and draws no conclusion about any contract.

## The read

There is no October reading of the lobster price index. The Bureau of Labor Statistics' public data service now returns October producer prices for the seafood index and the finished consumer foods index this record reads. It returns no October value for the producer price index for lobsters. The series still ends at September 2026, where it stood when Issue 1 was written: 198.8, which is 1.3% below September 2025 and 2.4% below September 2024. This record does not know why the month is absent, and BLS may publish it later.

The seafood index did move forward a month. The producer price index for all unprocessed and prepared seafood is 15.7% above October 2025 and 29.4% above October 2024. A supplier who says seafood costs are up 15.7% on the year is citing a real published series. It is not a lobster series, and this month there is no lobster reading for the same month to set beside it. The newest lobster reading is a month older and was lower on the year. Neither index settles what a particular supplier paid: the producer price index measures selling prices received by domestic producers, and these commodity indexes exclude imports.

Imports through the Portland, Maine customs district stayed low in volume and rose in declared value. September volume was 256,521 kg against 686,796 kg a year earlier, down 62.6%, and the January-to-September total is 16.8% below the same months of 2025, 14.2 million kg against 17.1 million. The average declared customs value was $9.64 a pound against $9.26, up 4.1%.

Maine landings and dock prices are unchanged from Issue 1, because the source file is the same one. Landings fell from 132.6 million pounds in 2016 to a preliminary 78.8 million in 2025, down 40.6%, while the annual ex-vessel price rose from $4.08 a pound to $5.85. That is a fact about Maine landed volume and the price paid at the Maine dock. It is not a measure of total market supply, and the long-term decline does not, by itself, establish an increase in any supplier's costs since the original quote. Year over year, the September dock price was 2.1% lower in 2025 than in 2024.

## The explanation

Two different gaps sit in this month's BLS figures, and they are not the same kind. The October 2025 consumer price readings are marked unavailable by BLS, with a stated reason. The October 2026 lobster reading is simply absent: the series returns September as its newest month, with no entry and no note for October, from the same service that returns October for the other producer price series this record reads. The October 2025 lobster reading exists, so the year-earlier side of the comparison is in the record and the current side is not.

Three measures describe different parts of the lobster market. They cover different populations, dates and product mixes, and nothing in this record establishes that they move together.

The ex-vessel price is what the boat gets at the Maine dock, and the most recent September figure is $6.15 a pound, from 2025 and marked preliminary. Import unit value is the average customs value declared on live lobster entering the Portland, Maine customs district, $9.64 a pound in September 2026; it excludes U.S. duties, international freight and insurance, so it is not a landed cost. At the counter, a single 1.25 lb hard shell ran $18.90 to $19.50 at two Maine sellers, which is $15.12 to $15.60 a pound at the stated minimum weight. The distance between the dock and the counter covers everything that happens in between. This record cannot itemize it or say how it is divided.

Seasonality moves the lobster index more than the year does. In 2025, and so far in 2026, the index peaked in March. The 2026 peak ran above 2025's, 334 in March against 315, a difference of 5.9%. By September the 2026 index stood 1.3% below September 2025. A March-to-October comparison crosses seasons and cannot isolate a year-over-year change, and this year the October end of that comparison has not been published.

What the import figures cannot settle is why the volume fell. Lower Canadian volume through one customs district can mean less supply, weaker demand, or lobster routed elsewhere, and this record cannot distinguish them. Maine's own 2026 landings are not in the published file, which ends with 2025. USDA's Food Price Outlook puts fish and seafood consumer prices 6.9% higher from September 2025 to September 2026, and gives a forecast interval for calendar 2027, against the 2026 average, of −2.3% to 7.6%. USDA does not forecast lobster separately.

Advertised consumer prices moved at four of the eight sellers between November 1 and November 15, in both directions. Seller B raised 17 multi-lobster pack listings by 6.7% to 23.1%, and seller E raised one listing by 5.0%. Seller H lowered three soft-shell per-pound listings by 9.1% to 20.0%, and seller D lowered two single soft-shell listings by 3.1% and 14.6%. The reference series, a single live hard-shell 1.25 lb lobster, did not move at either seller that lists it.

## The uncertainty

The lobster index in this issue is the September reading that Issue 1 carried. Nothing in this issue measures the lobster producer price for October, and the comparison between the lobster index and the seafood index is between different months. If BLS publishes the October lobster index, a later issue will carry it and say so.

The October producer price readings and the September lobster reading are preliminary, and BLS revises producer price indexes for up to four months after first publication. The September seafood reading has already been revised down since Issue 1 printed it; the September lobster reading has not changed.

This issue compares each series to the same period a year earlier and, where the record holds it, two years earlier. Month-to-month movement is not in this issue's computed set, so nothing here measures October against September.

The dock price is more than a year old. The Maine DMR file this issue uses ends with preliminary 2025 data, so the cited public series does not yet include 2026.

September import figures are recent and subject to revision, and trade data for recent months is routinely restated. The year-to-date fall of 16.8% covers nine months and is less sensitive to any single month than the 62.6% September figure. Both remain subject to revision. The record holds no September 2024 reading, so neither import measure carries a two-year comparison.

The producer price index for lobsters is an index, not a price. Its composition is not published in enough detail to say which products or channels drive it.

The consumer reference still rests on two sellers, D and G, because only two of the eight sellers observed list a single live hard-shell 1.25 lb lobster. Both were observed on both capture dates this month, D at $18.90 and G at $19.50, and neither moved. The wider set is fuller than last month's: all eight sellers yielded comparable live-lobster listings on both November dates, 152 series in all, of which 129 did not change advertised price and 23 did. The count includes listings shown as out of stock with a price, among them 15 of seller G's 17 on both dates and the one seller E listing that changed, which was out of stock on November 1. More than half of those series, 77, come from one seller, F, so the count describes listings and not the market. The changes are in advertised prices between two dates two weeks apart. They are not a monthly trend, and the consumer reference carries no year-over-year comparison: the observation record begins September 26, 2026.

The chain comparison mixes dates. The dock price is September 2025, the import value is September 2026, and the counter price is November 2026. The spread between them is illustrative and is not a margin calculation.

## The buyer's question

Ask the supplier three things before accepting a lobster-driven increase.

Which product: live, meat or tail; what size and shell; what grade. These move differently, and a quote for 1.25 lb hard shell cannot be checked against a claim about meat.

Which dates: the date the original quote priced from, and the date the adjustment references. A March-to-October comparison crosses seasons and cannot isolate a year-over-year change.

Which series: the published series or supplier invoice behind the stated increase. If the answer is that seafood prices are up, ask whether the series quoted is the seafood index or the lobster index. Both are free and public. If the claim cites an October lobster index, ask where the figure comes from: at this issue's retrieval, the BLS data service returned none.
