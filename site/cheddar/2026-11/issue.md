# Cheddar Check

**Issue 2: November 2026**
Field Assembly Market Publications · Information as of November 16, 2026 · Data run `2026-11-16T134220Z`

This is the second issue of Cheddar Check. It is written for a pizzeria, sandwich shop or caterer facing a supplier's price claim on cheddar. It reports market evidence. It does not estimate any supplier's cost and draws no conclusion about any contract.

## The read

Two block cheddar prices are in this issue. In the October issue they moved in different directions. Over the four weeks in this issue they again did not move alike: the mandatory-reported price rose every week, while the CME weekly average rose in the first week-to-week step and fell in the next two.

The mandatory-reported price, what cheese manufacturers received for 40-pound blocks sold in the week, was $1.6397 a pound for the week ending November 7, against $1.7959 in the comparable week of 2025, a fall of 8.7%. Its four-week average, $1.4930, is 15.5% lower than a year earlier. It rose week by week across those four weeks, from $1.3864 in the week ending October 17 to $1.4179, $1.5279 and $1.6397. The latest week stands well above its own four-week average because the three weeks before it were all lower; the average and the latest week are both correct, and they answer different questions. The weighted average rests on 10,097,100 pounds sold in the latest week, 7.9% fewer than a year earlier.

The CME cash market for cheese, which USDA reprints, went up and then came down over the same four weeks. Its weekly average for 40-pound blocks went from $1.5395 a pound in the week of October 12 to 16, the latest week in the October issue, to $1.7385 in the week to October 23, then to $1.7095 and to $1.5820 in the week of November 2 to 6. Blocks closed that Friday at $1.5675. The four-week average of the block weekly averages is $1.6424, so here the latest week stands below its four-week average. The CME barrel weekly average was $1.7200 in the latest week, after $1.5095, $1.7040 and $1.7230; its four-week average is $1.6641. This record began collecting the CME series in September and holds eight weeks of it, so it cannot say where the CME market stood a year ago.

This time the two prices reach the same week. In the mandatory week ending November 7, paired with the CME week ending November 6, the mandatory block price was $0.0577 a pound above the CME block weekly average. That is the arithmetic difference between two measures of the block market, not a margin. In each of the three earlier weeks of the four, the mandatory price was below the CME weekly average for the paired week.

The CME figures stop at November 6. The Dairy Market News file collected on November 13 and again on November 16 was still the report for the week of November 2 to 6, so the week of November 9 to 13 is not in this record, and this record does not forecast what it will show.

Only one of the five monthly cheese and food series has a year-earlier figure this month. The producer price index for natural cheese for October is 5.3% below October 2025. For the other four, the Bureau of Labor Statistics published no October 2025 value, so no year-over-year comparison exists: the consumer price index for cheese and related products, the consumer price index for food away from home, and the retail average prices of natural cheddar, $6.127 a pound in October, and of American processed cheese, $4.705 a pound.

What these figures establish is narrow. Against a year earlier, the mandatory block price and the natural cheese producer index are lower, so they do not support a general claim that cheddar prices rose on the year; the consumer indexes and the retail averages cannot be put to that test this month. Within the last four weeks, the mandatory block price rose every week, and a claim that "the block market went up" can point to that. In the week of November 2 to 6, the latest this record holds, the CME block weekly average was lower than in either of the two weeks before, though above the $1.5395 of the week of October 12 to 16; a claim that the block market has come off can point to that week and no later. Both are in the record at once. The figures do not establish what happened to any particular supplier's costs.

## The explanation

Five kinds of measure appear above and they answer different questions. The mandatory-reported block price is a weighted average of what reporting manufacturers received in a week. The CME figures are cash-market quotes, as USDA reprints them. The producer price index measures the selling prices received by domestic producers of natural cheese and excludes imports. The two consumer price indexes measure the change in prices urban consumers pay: one for cheese and related products at retail, one for meals and snacks bought away from home, which is not a restaurant's cost. The two average prices are retail prices per pound for two items. None of them is a supplier's cost, and a movement in one does not fix a movement in another.

The mandatory-reported price is narrower than the word "cheddar". The National Dairy Products Sales Report of November 12, 2026 defines it as cheddar cheese in 40-pound blocks, colored between 6 and 8 on the National Cheese Institute color chart, meeting Wisconsin State Brand, USDA Grade A, or better standards, and says 17 entities report it. The price is a weighted average in dollars per pound for the week ending Saturday. The report states no age, moisture content or pricing point; it refers its definitions to the program regulations (7 CFR 1170) and to the questionnaire manufacturers complete, and this record retains neither. It says it is subject to revision for the previous four weeks; in the November 12 report the week ending October 24 is marked revised, in both price and pounds, and the week ending October 17 was revised in an earlier report. It is not a price for cut, shredded or sliced cheese, for barrels, or for any other variety. A pizzeria buys more mozzarella than cheddar, and nothing here is a mozzarella price.

The CME figures are the CME Group cash market for cheese as USDA's Dairy Market News reprints it, in dollars per pound in carlot quantities. The weekly average is, in the report's words, "the simple average of the daily close prices for the calendar week," Monday to Friday, computed by Dairy Market News: an average of closing prices, not weighted by the number or size of trades. The reprint states no grade, color, age, moisture content or delivery point, and labels the barrel row only "Barrels", with no barrel weight. The two block measures need not move together. Over the four weeks in this issue the mandatory price, which averages a week's sales across reporting manufacturers, rose every week, while the cash quote rose in the first of its three week-to-week steps and fell in the next two. This record states the movements and not a reason for them.

The mandatory report and the CME reprint do not share a week-ending day. The mandatory report's week ends Saturday and the CME reprint's ends Friday, so the derived difference pairs the mandatory week ending November 7 with the CME week ending November 6, the nearest week within four days. In that pair the mandatory price, $1.6397, was above the CME weekly average, $1.5820. The same pairing for the three earlier weeks sets $1.3864 against $1.5395, $1.4179 against $1.7385, and $1.5279 against $1.7095, the mandatory price the lower of the two each time. A difference that changes sign inside a month is one more reason not to read it as anyone's margin.

USDA's Food Price Outlook, in its update of October 23, 2026, puts dairy product consumer prices 0.7% lower from September 2025 to September 2026, food away from home 3.5% higher and all food 2.5% higher. It gives forecast intervals for 2027 of −6.5% to 5.6% for dairy products, 0.7% to 5.0% for food away from home, and −2.5% to 6.2% for all food. The dairy interval runs from a fall to a rise.

## The uncertainty

Comparisons that do not exist in this issue, and why. Four monthly series have no year-over-year figure: the consumer price index for cheese and related products, the consumer price index for food away from home, and the retail average prices of natural cheddar and of American processed cheese. In the Bureau of Labor Statistics file this record retains, the October 2025 entry for each carries no value and the note "Data unavailable due to the 2025 lapse in appropriations". The value was not published; it is not zero, and this record does not fill it from a neighboring month. The producer price index for natural cheese has its October 2025 value, so its comparison stands. Four CME rows have no year-over-year figure either: the block and barrel weekly averages and their four-week averages. Each Dairy Market News report carries one week, so this record holds only the weeks it has collected since September 2026, eight at this issue's run, and a year-over-year figure will not exist until the record covers a year, around September 2027. The derived difference carries no year-earlier comparison because the CME series it subtracts has none in this record; the barrel series has printed no price since the week ending May 31, 2025, so it has nothing to compare.

The four-week averages are simple averages of four weekly figures, each week counting equally; the mandatory one is not weighted by pounds. Because the mandatory price rose in every one of its four weeks, its four-week average sits below its latest week. Its two changes on the year differ as well, 8.7% lower for the week and 15.5% lower for the average. Neither is the better number; a claim should say which it uses.

The CME figures are ten days older than this issue's run. The latest Dairy Market News report this record holds is for the week of November 2 to 6. The collections of November 13 and November 16 returned that same report, byte for byte, so the week of November 9 to 13 is absent. The mandatory report's latest week ends November 7; the record holds no later week of either series.

Where a Dairy Market News report prints two weekly averages for the same week, this record reads the first page. That matters for one of the four weeks averaged here: the report for the week of October 12 to 16 gives $1.5395 for blocks and $1.5095 for barrels on its first page, and the weekly average column of its second-page table prints different figures for the same week. The first page equals the simple average of the five daily closes printed in the second-page table, as the report's own definition requires. The reports for the three later weeks print the same weekly averages on both pages.

The 500-pound barrel series in the mandatory report is history, not a current price. It last printed a price for the week ending May 31, 2025, $1.9495 a pound, in the report of June 4, 2025. That figure is a first print: the report of June 11, 2025 carried that week and the three before it as empty, every week since is empty, and the current report's list of products it collects does not include barrels. This record reads the column named `cheese_500_Price`; the retained file also carries a column named `cheese_500_Wtd_Price` and does not say which of the two, if either, is adjusted to a moisture basis. The series is also a different measure from the CME "Barrels" quote above, which is current.

The latest mandatory week, ending November 7, is as first published, and the next four reports may revise it. A year-over-year comparison for the weekly series is against the week ending nearest 52 weeks earlier, within four days; here the week ending November 7, 2026 is compared with the week ending November 8, 2025.

The November 12 report describes its participants as 62 reporting entities "selling one million pounds or more of dairy products as defined by statute", 17 of them reporting 40-pound blocks. Mandatory reporting is not every sale of cheddar in the country, and the pounds sold are not total cheddar production.

The October producer price index is preliminary, and BLS may revise it for up to four months after first publication.

The two retail average prices are U.S. city averages for natural cheddar and American processed cheese. They are what shoppers paid at retail, not a foodservice price and not a price for any particular pack or brand.

The USDA Food Price Outlook figures are year-over-year from September 2025 to September 2026, not October to October. The forecast intervals are for 2027 and cover dairy products as a category, not cheese.

This issue's computed set carries one weekly figure from the October issue for each CME series, the week of October 12 to 16. The October issue's mandatory-reported figures and its derived difference fall outside this issue's four weeks and are not restated here; the October issue stays published as it was.

## The buyer's question

Ask the supplier three things before accepting a cheddar increase.

Which product and which market: the form and pack (block, barrel, shredded, sliced), and whether the price claim refers to the CME spot market or to mandatory-reported sales. They are different measures, and over the last four weeks they did not move alike: the mandatory block price rose every week to November 7, and the CME block weekly average rose to $1.7385 in the week to October 23 and was lower in each of the two weeks after.

Which dates: the week the original quote priced from and the week the adjustment references. The CME block weekly average was $1.7385 in one week and $1.5820 two weeks later; the mandatory block price was $1.3864 in one week and $1.6397 three weeks later. A quote priced from one of those weeks can sit some way from another.

Which series: the published series or supplier invoice behind the stated increase, and whether a comparison with last year is for a single week or an average. Every series above is free and public. Against a year earlier, the mandatory block price is 8.7% lower for the latest week and 15.5% lower on its four-week average, and the natural cheese producer index is 5.3% lower. A claim that rests on a year-over-year change in the cheese consumer price index or in a retail average price for October cannot be checked against this record, because the Bureau of Labor Statistics file it retains carries no October 2025 value for those series.
