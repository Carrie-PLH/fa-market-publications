02Specimen

What an issue contains

Every issue of every title has the same six parts, in the same order. The value is in connecting the evidence for one buyer, not in restating a government table. Excerpts below are from the Lobster Monitor baseline issue.

1The read

A short assessment of market conditions

What the evidence says, in a few paragraphs, with the seasonal shape named so that a quote from one month is not compared to a claim from another.

The 2026 winter peak ran higher than 2025's, then fell back to parity by August. A quote priced in March and an adjustment claimed in September compare two seasons, not two years.
2The numbers

Five to seven measures against the right comparison

Latest period, same period a year earlier, and the change, with the series identifier so a reader can pull the source themselves. The board above is this section.

3The explanation

How price, production, inventory, and trade relate

The chain from dock to counter, and what moves each link.

Maine's most recent September dock price is $6.15 a pound. Live Canadian imports entered Portland at $8.41 in July. A single 1¼ lb hard shell at the counter works out to about $15 a pound. The spread is handling, grading, mortality, and margin. It is wide, and it is where most of a buyer's price lives.
4The uncertainty

Conflicting signals, lags, gaps, and limits to the comparison

Stated, not buried. Which figures are preliminary, which months get revised, which comparisons mix dates, and where a series is thin.

The dock price is a year old. Maine publishes monthly landings once a year, in February. Any claim about 2026 dock prices in Maine rests on private data.
5The buyer's question

Something useful to put to a supplier

Which product: live, meat, or tail; what size and shell. Which dates: what the quote priced from, and what the adjustment references. Which evidence: what published series or invoice supports the number.
6The record

Sources, dates, methods, corrections

Every release used, its retrieval time and file hash, the formulas, and any correction to an earlier issue, left visible.

03Method

Public sources, retained evidence, dated claims

These publications extend Field Assembly's standing practice: first-party sources, quoted exactly and dated; every capture retained and externally timestamped; evidence kept apart from judgment; corrections made on the record.

Sources

Free government statistics, official market reports, trade-body publications, and a small set of public price observations. No paid data subscriptions. No product listings maintained by the hundred. Like is compared with like: grade, cut, size, unit, geography, and purchasing channel.

Evidence

Each issue ships with a preserved package: the source versions used, publication and retrieval dates, the calculations, and the issue as delivered. Files are hashed into an append-only chain anchored by RFC 3161 timestamp tokens and OpenTimestamps proofs.

Limits

A timestamp shows a captured record existed by a date. It does not show a source's figure was correct or that a quoted price was available to every buyer. Market evidence is not a supplier's cost, and no issue states whether a contractual adjustment is permitted.
