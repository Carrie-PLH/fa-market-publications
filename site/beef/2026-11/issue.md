# Beef Monitor

**Issue 2: November 2026**
Field Assembly Market Publications · Information as of November 16, 2026 · Data run `2026-11-16T134136Z`

This is the second issue of the Beef Monitor. It is written for a restaurant, steakhouse, burger business or caterer facing a supplier's price claim on beef. It reports market evidence. It does not estimate any supplier's cost and draws no conclusion about any contract.

## The read

Against a year earlier, the boxed beef cutout, fed cattle prices and the producer price index for slaughter cattle are lower, and the producer price index for beef cuts is higher. The four retail and consumer measures have no year-earlier figure this month, because the Bureau of Labor Statistics gives no value for them for October 2025. Four comparisons have changed direction since Issue 1, which reported the cutout above its year-earlier week, the beef cuts index below its year-earlier month, Choice further above Select than a year earlier and the cattle on feed inventory above a year earlier.

The Choice boxed beef cutout, USDA's composite value of boxed beef cuts from negotiated sales, was $359.84 per hundredweight in the week ending November 13, against $375.06 in the comparable week of 2025, 4.1% lower. Its four-week average, $365.48, is 2.8% lower than the $376.04 of the comparable four weeks a year earlier. The Select cutout was $350.14, against $357.80, 2.1% lower, with its four-week average, $354.09, 1.2% lower. Within those four weeks the Choice cutout fell from $370.55 in the week ending October 23 to $368.52, $363.02 and $359.84 in the three weeks after. The Select cutout stood between $355.05 and $355.98 for three weeks and then at $350.14. This issue's computed set compares only these four weeks with a year earlier; it does not show how earlier weeks compared.

Fed cattle prices are lower than a year earlier. The weighted average price of fed steers and heifers bought on a dressed basis, all grades together, was $360.59 in the report dated November 10, which covers the week ending Sunday, November 8, on 249,114 head. A year earlier it was $369.60, so the price is 2.4% lower; the report's own year-ago change is −$9.01. On a live basis the price was $222.95, against $230.32, 3.2% lower. The four-report averages are lower by 2.8% dressed and 3.3% live. Across the four reports the dressed price moved between $358.42 and $363.70 and the live price between $222.95 and $230.64, the latest live figure being the lowest of the four. The producer price index for slaughter cattle is 8.3% below October 2025.

The producer price index for beef primal and subprimal cuts made in slaughtering plants is 5.0% above October 2025, while the cutout is below its year-earlier week. These are different measures for different periods: the index is for the month of October and the cutout figures are for weeks ending October 23 to November 13. This issue's computed set holds no cutout figure for October as a month to set beside the index, and no September index value, so it cannot say how much of the change of direction since Issue 1 is the index's own movement and how much is the level it is compared with a year earlier.

Retail has levels this month and no year-earlier figure to compare them with. The average retail price of all uncooked ground beef was $7.068 a pound in October 2026 and of all uncooked beef steaks $12.845 a pound. The consumer price indexes for beef and veal and for food away from home have October 2026 values too. For all four, the Bureau of Labor Statistics marks October 2025 "Data unavailable due to the 2025 lapse in appropriations", so a comparison of October with October a year earlier does not exist for retail ground beef, retail beef steaks, the beef and veal consumer price index or the food away from home consumer price index. This record does not substitute another month. Each of the four does have an October 2024 value, and each is higher than it: ground beef by 20.1%, steaks by 21.6%, the beef and veal index by 20.9% and food away from home by 7.6%.

What these figures establish is narrow. A claim that beef prices rose on the year can point to one measure in this issue, the producer price index for beef cuts, 5.0% higher for October. The cutout does not support it: Choice is 4.1% lower and Select 2.1% lower than in the comparable week of 2025, and both four-week averages are lower. The retail and consumer measures cannot be read either way against October 2025, because that month was not published. So the answer depends on which wholesale measure and which dates a claim uses. The fed cattle prices and the slaughter cattle index are all lower than a year earlier, so an increase explained by cattle costs higher than a year ago finds no support in them. Against October 2024 the picture is different: every monthly beef measure with a two-year comparison stands well above it, the slaughter cattle index by 17.4%, the beef cuts producer index by 25.5%, the beef consumer index by 20.9%, ground beef by 20.1% and steaks by 21.6%. The figures do not establish what happened to any particular supplier's costs.

The Choice cutout less the dressed fed cattle price was $2.43, both figures labeled per hundredweight in this record, pairing the cutout week ending November 6 with the cattle report dated November 10. That pair is a week older than the cutout headline above, because the cattle report whose week overlaps the cutout week ending November 13 had not published when this issue's data was collected on November 16. Issue 1 reported a larger difference for its own pair, a month earlier; that figure is not in this issue's computed set and is not repeated here. The difference has no prior-year figure in this record, and it is not a margin: the cutout counts Choice beef from negotiated sales only, the cattle price counts all grades and negotiated, formula, contract and grid purchases together, and neither is anyone's cost. A smaller difference therefore does not show that anyone's margin narrowed. The explanation sets out the differences.

Choice stands closer to Select than a year ago. The Choice cutout less the Select cutout was $9.70 in the week ending November 13, against $17.26 in the comparable week of 2025.

The supply figures describe a smaller flow into and out of feedlots. USDA's estimate of cattle on feed in feedlots with capacity of 1,000 or more head was 11,372 thousand head on October 1, 0.4% fewer than a year earlier. Placements during September were 1,777 thousand head, 12.1% fewer, and marketings 1,524 thousand head, 6.6% fewer. NASS says both September figures were the lowest for September since the series began in 1996. A head count is not a measure of how much beef is produced, and the record holds no measure of demand, imports, exports or cold storage, so it cannot say what drove any of these prices.

## The explanation

Six kinds of measure appear above and they answer different questions. The cutout is a value USDA computes each week from negotiated sales of boxed beef cuts. The fed cattle price is a weighted average of what reported purchases of fed cattle paid in a week. The producer price indexes measure the selling prices received by domestic producers and exclude imports: one for cattle sold for slaughter, one for beef primal and subprimal cuts made in slaughtering plants. The two consumer price indexes measure the change in prices urban consumers pay: one for beef and veal at retail, one for meals and snacks bought away from home, which is not a restaurant's cost. The two average prices are retail prices per pound for two items. The NASS figures are counts of animals, not prices. None of them is a supplier's cost, and a movement in one does not fix a movement in another.

The cutout is narrower than the word "beef". Report 2461 states that it is "based on negotiated prices and volume of boxed beef cuts delivered within 0-21 days and on average industry cutting yields", in U.S. dollars per hundred pounds, and it lists the cuts behind it on an FOB plant basis, so freight to the buyer is not included. Choice and Select are separate columns, each headed 600-900; the report does not say what that range is measured in. The weekly figure is the report's weekly average, the simple average of the week's daily values, dated the last day of the week, here Friday, November 13. It counts negotiated sales only, not formula or contract sales, and it has columns for Choice and Select only, so it is not a value for Prime or ungraded beef. It is a computed value for a composite of cuts, not the price of any cut a kitchen orders: a strip loin, a chuck roll and ground beef each trade at their own prices in the same report. The pork cutout, as Pork Monitor Issue 1 describes it, is a different kind of measure: one comprehensive value across all sales types and all delivery periods. The weekly Cattle and Beef Summary also prints a comprehensive boxed beef cutout, a different figure from this one; that report is retained and not parsed, so it contributes no figure here.

The fed cattle price is report 2700's weighted average for all steers and heifers of beef type, total of all grades: lots more than sixty-five percent Choice and lots less than sixty-five percent Choice taken together, so lots with less than sixty-five percent Choice are in it. Dressed and live are separate series, and dairy-bred cattle, which the report lists separately, are not in it. The record reads the report's Datamart JSON, which states no covered week and no purchase type. The report's PDF does, and this issue's run retains the PDF of the report the figures come from, dated November 10, 2026. It heads the section "For week ending Sunday", giving that Sunday as November 8, 2026, and "Weekly Information from 2pm Previous Monday to 10am Current Monday; Includes Negotiated, Formula Nets, Contract Nets, & Grid Nets". It prints each price in dollars and states no weight basis. This record labels the price in dollars per hundredweight, a unit taken from its own source list rather than from either retained file. The date on each row is normally the day the report published, a Tuesday since March 31, 2026 and a Monday before that, not the end of the week it covers: the row dated November 10, 2026 covers the week ending Sunday, November 8, and the year-earlier row, dated Monday, November 10, 2025, covers the week ending Sunday, November 9, 2025.

The derived difference subtracts the dressed cattle price from the Choice cutout, $363.02 less $360.59. Because the cutout's week runs Monday to Friday and the cattle report's runs from Monday afternoon to the next Monday morning, each cutout week is paired with the cattle report dated the Tuesday after its Friday, whose week overlaps it: the cutout week ending Friday, November 6 with the report dated Tuesday, November 10. The report dated before that Friday covers purchases of the week before. The latest cutout week, ending November 13, has no cattle report to pair with in this issue, so the difference is a week older than the cutout headline. The two figures are not on the same terms: the cutout is Choice only and from negotiated boxed beef sales delivered within 0-21 days, while the cattle price covers all grades and negotiated, formula, contract and grid purchases; and neither retained report states that its figure is per hundred pounds of the same thing. The result is an arithmetic difference between two reported series. This record has no access to what it costs anyone to slaughter cattle and cut the carcass into boxes, so the difference is not a margin and says nothing about any packer's costs. It carries no prior-year figure here, so this issue cannot say whether it is wide or narrow by last year's standard. It is smaller than the difference Issue 1 reported for a pair a month earlier. Two pairs do not show a trend, and this issue's computed set holds no other pair, so it cannot say whether $2.43 is unusual.

The Choice less Select spread is a difference within one report, the same week: $9.70 in the week ending November 13, against $17.26 a year earlier. The report prints the same figure as its "Choice/Select Spread". The table shows its change as a percentage, 43.8%, but a percentage change on a difference is sensitive when the difference is small, so the two levels are the better read: Choice is closer to Select than a year ago. It was closer still the week before, when the Choice cutout was $363.02 and the Select cutout $355.17.

The retail and consumer rows are levels only. In the response this record retains, the Bureau of Labor Statistics gives no value for October 2025 in four of the six series this title reads, the two average prices and the two consumer price indexes, and marks each "Data unavailable due to the 2025 lapse in appropriations". The two producer price indexes do carry October 2025 values, so their comparisons on the year stand. Issue 1 reported all four retail and consumer measures higher than a year earlier for September; this issue cannot say whether that held in October, only that each October 2026 value is above October 2024.

USDA's Food Price Outlook, in its update of October 23, 2026, puts beef and veal consumer prices 3.5% higher from September 2025 to September 2026, meats 1.3% higher, food away from home 3.5% higher and all food 2.5% higher. It gives forecast intervals for 2027 of −8.8% to 15.5% for beef and veal, −8.7% to 10.0% for meats, and 0.7% to 5.0% for food away from home. The beef and veal interval is wide and runs from a fall to a rise.

## The uncertainty

This is the second issue of this title. It compares each series with the same period a year earlier and, where the record holds it, two years earlier. Where it compares with Issue 1 it gives the direction only, because Issue 1's figures are not in this issue's computed set. Month-to-month movement is not in this issue's computed set, except where the text quotes consecutive weekly values from it.

Four monthly series have no year-earlier value. The Bureau of Labor Statistics gives no October 2025 value for the beef and veal consumer price index, the food away from home consumer price index or the average retail prices of ground beef and beef steaks, and gives the 2025 lapse in appropriations as the reason. For those four, this issue has an October 2026 level and a comparison with October 2024 and nothing between. A two-year change does not show when within the two years the change happened.

The weekly series do not share a week. The cutout headline is the week ending Friday, November 13. The fed cattle headline is the report dated Tuesday, November 10, covering the week ending Sunday, November 8. The cutout less cattle difference pairs the cutout week ending November 6 with that report, a week older than the cutout headline. A reader setting the beef cutout beside the pork cutout in Pork Monitor is comparing different kinds of measure and should check which week each issue reports.

The fed cattle dates in this record are publication dates, not week endings. The covered week is the one ending the Sunday before the date shown. The 2025 rows were reloaded in the Datamart on March 24, 2026, so the record does not show when they first published or whether they are first prints; their covered week is inferred from the report's 2026 wording.

Report 2700 prints its prices in dollars with no weight basis; the per-hundredweight label is this record's, from its source list.

A year-over-year comparison for the weekly series is against the week nearest 52 weeks earlier, within four days. The cutout week ending November 13, 2026 is compared with the week ending November 14, 2025; the cattle report of November 10, 2026 with the report of November 10, 2025, which was published on a Monday and covers the week ending Sunday, November 9, 2025. The four-week figures are simple averages of four weekly values, each week counting equally. Weighting those weeks by the head or loads traded behind them could give a different number.

Mandatory price reporting covers trade at reporting plants. It is not every transaction in the country, and the negotiated sales behind the cutout are only part of the boxed beef trade.

The October producer price indexes are preliminary, and BLS may revise them for up to four months after first publication. The September indexes Issue 1 reported were preliminary too, and the response retained by this issue's run carries a September value for the beef cuts index that differs from the one Issue 1 reported.

The cattle on feed figures are from the NASS release of October 23, 2026: the inventory on October 1, and placements and marketings during September. They cover feedlots with capacity of 1,000 or more head only. Outside January and July the survey covers feedlots in 16 States, and NASS says the previous month's estimates are subject to revision each month and reviews the previous year's in February, so the 2025 figures here are the ones the October 2026 release prints and may differ from what was first published. The October release also revises the September 1 inventory and the August placements and marketings that Issue 1 reported from the September release; the revised figures are not in this issue's computed set. The record holds no 2024 reading, so these figures carry no two-year comparison. The release's opening sentence counts "cattle and calves on feed"; its definitions describe steers and heifers fed for the slaughter market and expected to grade Select or better.

The two retail average prices are U.S. city averages for all uncooked ground beef and all uncooked beef steaks. They are what shoppers paid at retail, not a foodservice price and not a price for a particular cut, grade or pack.

The USDA Food Price Outlook figures are year-over-year from September 2025 to September 2026. They are the outlook's own figures for an earlier month, not a substitute for the October comparison this issue lacks. The forecast intervals are for 2027. They cover beef and veal, meats and food away from home as categories.

The cutout and the fed cattle price are USDA figures built from reported trade, not an offer available to any particular buyer, and a restaurant's delivered cost includes processing, freight and terms this record does not see.

## The buyer's question

Ask the supplier three things before accepting a beef increase.

Which product and which basis: the cut, its grade (Prime, Choice, Select or ungraded), how it is trimmed and packed, and whether it is fresh or frozen. The cutout above is a composite value for Choice or for Select, built from cuts sold FOB plant, not the price of a case of any product, and in the latest week it was 4.1% lower than a year earlier for Choice and 2.1% lower for Select.

Which dates: the date the original quote priced from and the date the adjustment references. The Choice cutout went from $370.55 per hundredweight in the week ending October 23 to $359.84 in the week ending November 13. If the claim cites the cattle market, ask which report: a fed cattle report is dated the Tuesday it publishes and covers the week before.

Which series: the published series or supplier invoice behind the stated increase. Every series above is free and public. If the increase is put down to cattle costs, the fed cattle prices and the slaughter cattle index in this issue are lower than a year earlier: the dressed fed cattle price 2.4%, the live price 3.2% and the slaughter cattle producer index 8.3%. Against October 2024 the slaughter cattle index is 17.4% higher, so the comparison date the claim uses matters. If the claim cites retail or consumer beef prices against a year ago for October, ask which figure it compares with: the Bureau of Labor Statistics gives no October 2025 value for the retail and consumer series this record reads.
