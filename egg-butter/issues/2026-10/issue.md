# Egg & Butter Brief

**Issue 1: October 2026**
Field Assembly Market Publications · October 18, 2026 · Data run `2026-10-18T144956Z`

This is the first issue of the Egg & Butter Brief. It is written for a bakery, pastry shop or breakfast business facing a supplier's price claim on eggs or butter. It reports market evidence. It does not estimate any supplier's cost and draws no conclusion about any contract.

## The read

Both ingredients are a long way below where they were a year ago, and eggs are further below than almost any food series gets. The producer price index for chicken eggs is 75.3% below September 2025 and 82.3% below September 2024. Against its highest month in the past two years, February 2025, it is down 93.1%. The producer price index for butter is 32.0% below September 2025 and 55.7% below September 2024.

Retail has come down too, but nothing like as far. The consumer price index for eggs is 20.6% below September 2025 against the producer index's 75.3%; for butter, 10.3% against 32.0%. These are different measures and not two readings of one price, so the gap between them is not a margin and this record cannot say what it is made of. What it does establish is the direction of the question: a buyer told that egg or butter costs have risen is being told something the public series do not show, in either index, at either level.

Supply is the part of the picture that moved with prices. Layers averaged 382,801 thousand birds in August 2026, 4.6% above August 2025, and table egg production reached 8,121.6 million eggs, 6.0% higher. More eggs, and a much lower producer price, are consistent. This record does not establish that one caused the other.

## The explanation

Four kinds of measure appear above and they answer different questions. The producer price indexes measure selling prices received by domestic producers and exclude imports. The consumer price indexes measure what shoppers pay at retail. The mandatory dairy report and the shell egg report are surveyed transaction prices in named markets at a stated week. The NASS figures are counts of birds and eggs, not prices at all. None of them is a supplier's cost, and a movement in one does not fix a movement in another.

Eggs are the larger move by a wide margin. The producer index stood at 89.9 in September 2026 against 1,302.3 at its February 2025 peak, which is the highest month the past two years hold. This record documents the level and the date. It does not establish what drove either.

The three shell egg markets this issue reports all read lower in the week ending October 17 than the week before, as the report states them: national delivered warehouse 66.19 against 74.19 cents a dozen, Midwest paid to producers 38.00 against 46.00, and Northeast delivered warehouse 67.00 against 75.00. One week against the week before is a short base. The trailing four-week averages, 71.81 and 44.50 cents for the first two markets, sit above the latest week in both, which is what a falling series looks like, and four weeks is still a short base.

Butter is lower on the year at both the wholesale and the retail level, and the volume behind the wholesale figure fell with it: 2,780,060 pounds sold in the week ending October 10, 11.3% below the comparable week of 2025. Lower price and lower volume together can mean several things and this record cannot distinguish them. USDA's Food Price Outlook puts egg consumer prices 23.0% lower from August 2025 to August 2026 and dairy products 0.3% lower, and gives forecast intervals for calendar 2027 against the 2026 average of −30.3% to 47.8% for eggs and −5.0% to 8.4% for dairy. The egg interval is wide enough to be worth reading as the honest statement of uncertainty it is.

## The uncertainty

This is the first issue of this title, and several series carry no year-over-year comparison yet. The CME butter reprint and the three shell egg markets state one period per report, so this record accumulates them a week at a time and holds five weeks. Those rows show a latest week, a prior week as the report states it, and where four weeks exist a trailing average. They will carry a year-over-year figure when the record holds a year.

This issue compares each series to the same period a year earlier and, where the record holds it, two years earlier. Month-to-month movement is not in this issue's computed set, so nothing here measures September against August.

The NASS layer and production figures are for August 2026, the newest month that report publishes, and the record holds no August 2024 reading, so neither carries a two-year comparison.

The USDA Food Price Outlook figures are year-over-year from August 2025 to August 2026, not September to September, and the forecast intervals are for the calendar 2027 average against the 2026 average. They cover eggs and dairy products as categories, not butter on its own.

Report 2848 covers more markets than the three reported here: the record holds Large white quotations for seven markets, including Midwest delivered store door, South Central and Southeast, and the three computed in this issue were chosen as a national reading, a producer-level reading and a Northeast reading. A buyer in another region should read the market that matches where they buy.

The shell egg prices and the mandatory butter price are surveyed prices in named markets, not an offer available to any particular buyer, and a bakery's delivered cost includes terms this record does not see.

## The buyer's question

Ask the supplier three things before accepting an egg or butter increase.

Which market: shell eggs bought at the Midwest producer level, delivered to a warehouse, or broken and liquid; butter bought as bulk wholesale, printed retail packs, or a blend. These are quoted in different places and move by different amounts, and the figures above are shell eggs and bulk butter.

Which dates: the date the original quote priced from, and the date the adjustment references. A series this far below its own recent peak can still be above a quote priced at the bottom.

Which series: the published series or supplier invoice behind the stated increase. Every series above is free and public, and this month none of them shows eggs or butter higher than a year ago at either the producer or the retail level.
