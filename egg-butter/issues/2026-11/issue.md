# Egg & Butter Brief

**Issue 2: November 2026**
Field Assembly Market Publications · November 16, 2026 · Data run `2026-11-16T134103Z`

This is the second issue of the Egg & Butter Brief. It is written for a bakery, pastry shop or breakfast business facing a supplier's price claim on eggs or butter. It reports market evidence. It does not estimate any supplier's cost and draws no conclusion about any contract.

## The read

The egg evidence this month comes in two parts, and a buyer should keep them apart. The producer price index for chicken eggs for October 2026 is 74.4% below October 2025 and 79.5% below October 2024. Against its highest month in the past two years, February 2025, it is down 93.6%. The weekly shell egg quotations are a separate measure. They reach two weeks into November, past the index's reference month, and they rose week on week. The national delivered warehouse price for Large caged white eggs is 99.19 cents a dozen in the week ending November 14, against 80.19 cents the week before and 64.19 cents in each of the two weeks before that. It is the highest of the nine weeks this record holds.

Both statements are true at once and neither cancels the other. An October index far below a year earlier says nothing about the second week of November. Two weeks of rising quotations do not tell a buyer where eggs stand against a year ago, because this record holds nine weeks of the shell egg report and has no year-earlier week to set beside 99.19 cents. A supplier citing the November quotations is citing a real movement in a published series for caged white eggs in cartons. This record does not establish what caused it and cannot say whether it will last.

Butter prices are lower on the year on every price measure that carries a year-over-year comparison; reported volume is mixed, 1.6% lower for the single week and 8.9% higher on the four-week average. The producer price index for butter is 18.9% below October 2025 and 49.4% below October 2024. The mandatory wholesale price was $1.3560 a pound in the week ending November 7, 18.4% below the comparable week of 2025, and its four-week average of $1.3629 is 19.7% below the comparable four weeks a year earlier. The CME Grade AA butter weekly average fell 1.9% in the week ending November 6, to $1.2895 from $1.3145. These year-over-year falls are smaller than the ones the October issue reported. That is not evidence that the butter producer index rose: its October reading is lower than September's in the same retained file, and the month it is compared with, October 2025 at 117.9, is itself lower than the September 2025 figure the October issue compared with.

The retail comparison the October issue made cannot be made this month. The Bureau of Labor Statistics published no October 2025 figure for the three consumer price series this record holds; the series mark that month "Data unavailable due to the 2025 lapse in appropriations." The consumer indexes for eggs, butter and food away from home therefore carry no year-over-year figure in this issue. Against October 2024, which the record does hold, the egg consumer index is 16.2% lower where the egg producer index is 79.5% lower, and the butter consumer index is 10.6% lower where the butter producer index is 49.4% lower. As the October issue said, these are different measures and not two readings of one price, so the gap between them is not a margin.

The supply counts are for September. Total layers averaged 385,065 thousand birds in September 2026, 4.5% above September 2025, and table egg production was 7,931.6 million eggs, 6.5% higher. They sit beside an October index and November quotations, and this record does not establish a link between any of them.

## The explanation

Four kinds of measure appear above and they answer different questions. The producer price indexes measure selling prices received by domestic producers and exclude imports. The consumer price indexes measure what shoppers pay at retail. The mandatory dairy report and the shell egg report are surveyed transaction prices for named products in named markets at a stated week. The NASS figures are counts of birds and eggs, not prices at all. None of them is a supplier's cost, and a movement in one does not fix a movement in another.

The two weekly series are narrower than the words "eggs" and "butter" suggest. Report 2848 quotes caged white eggs in cartons, and its own note describes the quotations as prices to retailers on sales to volume buyers; the Midwest paid to producers row is quoted FOB. The mandatory butter price covers salted butter at eighty percent butterfat, fresh or storage, in 25-kilogram and 68-pound boxes meeting USDA Grade AA standards, and the volume beside it is the pounds that qualified for that report rather than all butter sold in the country. A bakery buying cage-free eggs, unsalted butter, or a higher-butterfat European-style butter is buying something these two series do not price.

The egg index and the egg quotations are different things at different dates. The index stood at 82.9 in October 2026 against 323.3 in October 2025 and 1,302.3 at its February 2025 peak, the highest month the past two years hold. It is an index of what domestic producers received for chicken eggs, with October 2026 as its reference month, not a price for any one size or pack, and the October figure is preliminary: the Bureau of Labor Statistics marks the four most recent months as subject to revision.

The three shell egg markets this issue reports all read higher in the week ending November 14 than the week before, as the report states them: national delivered warehouse 99.19 against 80.19 cents a dozen, Midwest paid to producers 71.00 against 52.00 cents, and Northeast delivered warehouse 100.00 against 81.00 cents. Each is the highest of the nine weeks this record holds for that market. The report carries Large white quotations for seven markets, and all seven are higher than the week before. The four weeks behind each average show the path: the national series read 64.19, 64.19, 80.19 and 99.19 cents, the Midwest producer series 36.00, 36.00, 52.00 and 71.00 cents, and the Northeast series 65.00, 65.00, 81.00 and 100.00 cents. Because two of those four weeks came before the rise, the four-week averages of 76.94, 48.75 and 77.75 cents sit below the latest week in all three markets. In the October issue the latest week sat below the two four-week averages it reported. A quote tied to a four-week average and a quote tied to the latest week start from different numbers, in either direction.

Butter is lower on the year at the producer and wholesale levels. The mandatory price of $1.3560 a pound in the week ending November 7 compares with $1.6627 in the week ending November 8, 2025, and the four-week average of $1.3629 with $1.6977. The CME reading is a separate market: a cash exchange average for Grade AA butter as reprinted by USDA, $1.2895 for the week ending November 6 with a Friday close of $1.2650.

Butter volume gives two readings. Reported qualifying sales were 3,366,906 pounds in the week ending November 7, 1.6% below the comparable week of 2025. The four-week simple average of 3,008,763 pounds is 8.9% above the 2,762,945 pounds of the same four weeks a year earlier. The single week and the four-week average point in different directions, which is a reason not to rest a statement about volume on either one alone.

USDA's Food Price Outlook puts egg consumer prices 20.6% lower from September 2025 to September 2026, the same September-to-September reading the October issue took from the consumer index itself, and dairy products 0.7% lower. Its forecast intervals for calendar 2027 against the 2026 average are −29.5% to 41.1% for eggs and −6.5% to 5.6% for dairy products. The egg interval remains wide enough to be read as a statement of uncertainty rather than a direction.

## The uncertainty

Several comparisons do not exist in this issue, and each is named here rather than left as a blank cell.

The consumer price indexes for eggs, butter and food away from home have no year-over-year comparison. The Bureau of Labor Statistics did not publish October 2025 for any of the three; the retained series files carry the note "Data unavailable due to the 2025 lapse in appropriations" for that month. The two producer price indexes do carry October 2025, so their year-over-year figures stand. The two-year comparisons to October 2024 exist for all five series.

The three shell egg markets have no year-over-year comparison, for the single week or for the four-week average. Report 2848 states one week and the prior week per report, so this record accumulates it a week at a time and holds nine weeks, from the week ending September 19 to the week ending November 14, 2026. Those rows show a latest week, the prior week as the report states it, and a simple average of four weeks. They will carry a year-over-year figure when the record holds a year.

The CME butter reprint has no year-over-year comparison either. The record holds eight weeks of it, from the week ending September 18 to the week ending November 6, 2026, and the row compares the latest week with the week before. It is also a week older than it would normally be: the Dairy Market News file retrieved on November 13 and again on November 16 was still the report for the week of November 2 to 6, so this record holds no CME reading for the week ending November 13.

The NASS figures are for September 2026, the newest month that report publishes, and the record holds no September 2024 reading, so neither carries a two-year comparison.

Sources restate their own figures, and figures from two sources that the October issue reported have since been restated. USDA has restated the mandatory butter price and pounds for the week ending October 10, both higher than first published, and has also restated the week ending October 3, one of the four weeks behind the October issue's four-week average. The restated October 10 pounds are above the year-earlier week the October issue compared them with, where the first-published pounds were below it. The NASS release this issue retains gives August 2026 layer and table egg figures higher than the ones the October issue reported from the prior release, which is retained by that issue's run and listed in its `evidence.json`, not this one's. The restated figures are not in this issue's computed set and are not quoted here; the retained captures hold them. The same applies forward: the mandatory report restates each week in the following four reports, so the week ending November 7 is a first-published figure, and the report marks its figure for the week ending October 17 as revised. The October producer price indexes are preliminary.

This issue compares each series to the same period a year earlier and, where the record holds it, two years earlier. Month-to-month movement is not in this issue's computed set. The one statement above that the butter producer index is lower in October than in September was read from the retained series file and gives a direction only.

The four-week figures are simple averages of four weekly values, each week counting equally. Weighting the butter weeks by the pounds sold behind them would give a different number. The rows are labeled for the method used.

The layer count is the total average number of layers during the month, which includes hens producing hatching eggs rather than table eggs. The table egg production figure beside it counts only table eggs. The two are not a matched pair, and this record does not parse the separate table-egg flock the same report publishes.

Report 2848 covers more markets than the three reported here: the record holds Large white quotations for seven markets, including Midwest delivered warehouse, Midwest delivered store door, South Central and Southeast, and the three computed in this issue were chosen as a national reading, a producer-level reading and a Northeast reading. A buyer in another region should read the market that matches where they buy.

The USDA Food Price Outlook figures are year-over-year from September 2025 to September 2026, not October to October, and the forecast intervals are for the calendar 2027 average against the 2026 average. They cover eggs and dairy products as categories, not butter on its own.

The shell egg prices and the mandatory butter price are surveyed prices in named markets, not an offer available to any particular buyer, and a bakery's delivered cost includes terms this record does not see.

## The buyer's question

Ask the supplier three things before accepting an egg or butter increase.

Which product: caged white shell eggs or cage-free; salted bulk butter at eighty percent butterfat or unsalted, higher-fat or printed retail packs. These are quoted in different places and move by different amounts, and the weekly figures above are caged white eggs and salted bulk butter.

Which dates: the date the original quote priced from, and the date the adjustment references. This month the answer matters for eggs. The October producer price index for chicken eggs is 74.4% below October 2025. The national Large quotation is 99.19 cents a dozen in the week ending November 14, against 64.19 cents three weeks before. These are different measures and different comparisons, not one price at two dates; a claim can be accurate about one and silent about the other.

Which series: the published series or supplier invoice behind the stated increase, and whether it is a single week or an average. Every series above is free and public. The latest shell egg week is above its four-week average in all three markets reported here, and no weekly egg series in this record can yet be compared with a year ago.
